# Physics-inspired tunneling through combinatorial barriers in VLSI placement

The most actionable of the three frameworks is **Framework 2 (Least Action / Mountain Pass)**, specifically a discrete Gentlest Ascent Dynamics analog guided by LP dual variables — an approach that appears genuinely novel in its synthesis. Framework 1 (Complexification) is theoretically elegant but computationally infeasible in its pure form; its practical residue is **parametric LP** and **solution-space smoothing**. Framework 3 (Quantum Tunneling) is largely ruled out for your barrier geometry — wide barriers of ~100+ flips defeat quantum-inspired methods — but **Population Annealing** emerges as the most implementable general-purpose tool. The hard constraint of ~30 LP evaluations per benchmark is the dominant design factor: it eliminates all methods requiring thousands of function evaluations and points toward surrogate-model-based or dual-guided strategies.

The three frameworks are not competitors. They converge on a single operational principle: **identify soft degrees of freedom via LP duality, and navigate through them**. This is the dimer method's "softest mode," the mountain pass theorem's "lowest saddle," and statistical mechanics' "unfrozen spins" — all the same object viewed through different lenses.

## Complexification is beautiful mathematics but computationally intractable

The topological argument is correct: in ℝ^{2N}, hyperplane arrangements disconnect the feasible region into exponentially many polyhedra; in ℂ^{2N}, Zariski's 1962 theorem guarantees the complement of complex codimension-1 hyperplanes is path-connected. Paths between any two feasible assignments exist through complex space. The problem is that no computational method can exploit this at scale.

**Homotopy continuation software** — PHCpack (Verschelde, version 2.4.92), Bertini 2.0, and HomotopyContinuation.jl (Breiding & Timme, 2018) — operates on polynomial systems of **4 to 20 variables** in practice. The fundamental bottleneck is the number of solution paths, which grows exponentially: ten quadratics yield 1,024 paths; twenty quadratics yield over one million. For 500 variables with 100K constraints, the path count is astronomically beyond reach. The largest systems solved by these tools involve moderate variable counts with exploited sparsity (polyhedral homotopy reducing path count via mixed volume), but nothing approaching hundreds of variables in general position. GPU parallelization (PHCpack) helps track paths concurrently but cannot overcome the exponential path-count explosion.

No application of homotopy continuation to combinatorial optimization at this scale exists in the literature. The closest work is Homotopy-Continuation Branch-and-Bound (HCBB) for mixed-integer nonlinear programming, which handles ~8,600 continuous variables but only **13 binary variables** — the discrete component is trivial.

**Parametric LP is the practical residue of complexification.** Instead of tracking paths through ℂ^{2N}, one can rotate a single separation constraint parametrically (from direction L to direction R, for instance) and track the LP optimal basis as it changes. This is classical single-parameter parametric LP, well-developed since Gass & Saaty (1955). For right-hand-side parameterization, the solution is piecewise affine, and the number of basis changes along a single-parameter path is bounded. For constraint-matrix changes (which is what rotating a separation direction entails), the theory is harder — the feasible region shape changes — but each basis change requires only a single LP pivot, potentially staying within a ~30-evaluation budget. The most recent algorithmic advance is Kenefake & Pistikopoulos (2024), a parallel combinatorial algorithm for multiparametric programming. The primary application domain remains Model Predictive Control, where the parametric LP solution is precomputed as an explicit piecewise-affine map. For your problem, a single-parameter sweep rotating one macro pair's separation constraint could cross polyhedron boundaries while tracking the optimal basis — a computationally tractable operation that costs O(number of basis changes) LP pivots per sweep.

**Gaussian homotopy** (Mobahi & Fisher, 2015) offers another practical angle. The Single-Loop Gaussian Homotopy method (SLGH, Iwakiri et al., NeurIPS 2022) eliminates the expensive double-loop structure of classical graduated optimization and supports zeroth-order (gradient-free) evaluation. Power-Transformed Gaussian Homotopy (2025) adds convergence guarantees. However, the zeroth-order variant requires O(d) function evaluations per step — at d = 500, this blows past the 30-evaluation budget in a single iteration. The closest VLSI application is **solution-space smoothing** (Dong et al., IEEE GLSVLSI 2003), which solves simplified instances first (fewer modules, relaxed constraints) and uses solutions to guide harder instances — exactly the continuation idea applied to floorplanning, tested on MCNC benchmarks.

The most intriguing discrete analog of complexification is **simulated lifting** (Shen et al., arXiv 2602.05887, February 2026): over-parameterization via tensor lifting converts local minima into strict saddle points. Their Simulated Oracle Direction mechanism projects escape directions from the lifted space back to the original space *without actually performing the lift*. This is conceptually close to complexification — enlarging the space to improve the landscape, then projecting back — but is currently developed only for matrix sensing, not combinatorial optimization.

## The mountain pass offers the most novel and actionable framework

Framework 2 produces the clearest operational algorithm among the three. Its core methods — the dimer method, NEB, GAD, and the string method — were all developed for continuous potential energy surfaces in computational chemistry. **None has been directly adapted to discrete combinatorial optimization.** Yet the conceptual mapping is precise, and the resulting discrete algorithm is both simple and well-motivated.

**Gentlest Ascent Dynamics** (Weinan E & Xiang Zhou, 2011) is the most conceptually aligned method. GAD constructs a dynamical system whose stable fixed points are exactly index-1 saddle points: it tracks the lowest eigenmode of the Hessian, reverses the force component along it (ascending along the softest direction), and follows the force perpendicular to it (descending in all other directions). Extensions include Constrained GAD (Liu, Xie & Yuan, 2022) for constrained saddle points, GAD on manifolds (Bello-Rivas et al., 2023), and High-index Saddle Dynamics (Yin et al., 2019–2025) for higher-index saddle points. GAD typically requires **50–200 force evaluations** in chemical systems.

The discrete analog for your problem is immediate and requires only **2 LP solves per step**: solve the LP to obtain dual variables, identify the pair with the smallest |dual| (the "softest mode"), flip that pair's L/R/A/B assignment, and re-solve. This traverses the assignment graph by ascending along the gentlest direction — the LP-dual-guided discrete GAD. With ~30 LP evaluations, this allows ~15 steps: enough to climb over a moderate saddle and descend into a new basin. The algorithm has a natural two-phase structure:

- **Phase 1 (ascent):** Flip soft pairs (small |duals|) sequentially. Cost rises, but slowly — you are climbing the gentlest slope toward the saddle.
- **Phase 2 (descent):** Once cost begins decreasing after a flip, you have crossed the saddle. Switch to greedy descent (flip the pair whose flipping reduces cost most).

**The soft-mode insight — that saddle points should be crossed by flipping small-dual pairs, not large-dual ones — is genuinely novel in its synthesis.** Three independent literatures support it without combining it:

- **Reduced cost fixing** (established in integer programming): variables with large reduced costs are provably fixable at their LP values. The contrapositive — variables with small reduced costs are flexible and should be explored — is the same insight. Hougardy & Schroeder (2024, Mathematical Programming Computation) used reduced-cost-based edge elimination for TSP, reducing graphs to under 3n edges by eliminating high-reduced-cost edges.
- **Backbone theory** (Slaney & Walsh, 2001; Zeng et al., 2012): backbone variables are frozen in all optimal solutions. Non-backbone (soft) variables define the search space for exploration. Backbone-guided extremal optimization (BGEO) for MAX-SAT operationalizes exactly this principle.
- **Survey propagation's joker variables**: in the spin glass theory of random K-SAT, "joker" or "white" variables are not frozen in any solution cluster. Backtracking Survey Propagation (Marino, Parisi & Ricci-Tersenghi, Nature Communications 2016) finds solutions by assigning joker variables first — the easy variables, not the hard ones.

The connection between LP duals and message-passing in spin glass physics is rigorous, not merely analogical. **Max-product belief propagation operates as block coordinate ascent on the LP dual** (Sontag et al., UAI 2008; Sanghavi et al. for weighted matching). LP dual variables *are* BP messages in the zero-temperature limit. The soft-mode insight thus connects to replica symmetry breaking: in the RSB phase, the solution space shatters into clusters, and the LP relaxation becomes loose — fractional LP optima represent superpositions across clusters. Dual variables with small magnitude signal variables that are unresolved between clusters: precisely the joker/soft variables.

**Minimax paths on the implicitly-defined assignment graph** formalize the mountain pass. The minimax path between two assignments σ₁ and σ₂ minimizes max_{t} E(σ(t)) over all flip-sequences connecting them — this is the Mountain Pass Theorem's saddle value. On explicit graphs, modified Dijkstra (replacing sum with max) or minimum spanning tree methods solve this in near-linear time (Hu, 1961). On implicit exponential graphs, no off-the-shelf algorithm exists. The LP-dual-guided greedy approach is a principled heuristic: at each step, it flips the pair minimizing barrier height, using duals as a local gradient. With ~15 steps, it traces an approximate minimax path through a local neighborhood of the assignment graph.

The Elastic String Algorithm (Moré & Munson, 2002, Argonne National Lab) is the most direct algorithmic implementation of the Mountain Pass Theorem for continuous spaces — it optimizes breakpoints of a piecewise-linear path to minimize maximum elevation. For discrete spaces, the LP-dual-guided approach is more practical.

## Quantum tunneling fails for wide barriers but Population Annealing works

The quantum-inspired framework faces a fundamental physics limitation. The Crosson-Harrow FOCS 2016 result shows SQA achieves exponential speedup over classical SA only for **thin, high barriers** where barrier width ζ and height α satisfy α + ζ < 1/2. For your ~100+ flip-wide barriers, the instanton action S ∝ width × height yields an astronomically small tunneling amplitude exp(−S). Nannini et al. (2023) confirm explicitly: "quantum annealing only performs well in exploring functions with high and narrow peaks, while classical annealing is better in overcoming flat and wide energy-profile barriers." **No results from 2022–2026 change this picture.** SQA is definitively the wrong tool for your barrier geometry.

**Discrete Schrödinger Bridge methods** — DASBS (Guo et al., February 2026), DDSBM (Kim et al., ICLR 2025), and SDDS (Sanokowski et al., ICLR 2025) — are theoretically exciting but impractical for LP-based energy functions. These methods learn a continuous-time Markov chain transport from a simple prior to a Boltzmann target distribution. SDDS uses REINFORCE (policy gradient), requiring only black-box energy evaluations — no differentiability needed. DASBS has been tested on **4-state Potts models on 16×16 lattices** (256 variables), directly relevant to your formulation. The fatal constraint is training cost: all methods require **10⁵ to 10⁶ energy evaluations** during neural network training. With each evaluation costing an LP solve taking seconds, the total training time is prohibitive.

**Population Annealing (PA)** emerges as the clear practical winner from Framework 3. PA maintains a population of R replicas cooled simultaneously: at each temperature step, replicas are resampled proportionally to Boltzmann weight ratios, then each undergoes local MCMC sweeps. Wang, Machta & Katzgraber (2015) benchmarked PA on 3D Edwards-Anderson spin glasses (up to N = 512) and found it **significantly more efficient than SA and comparable to parallel tempering (PT)**. PA's key advantage is embarrassing parallelism — all replicas are independent between resampling steps. For wide barriers, PA overcomes them by maintaining diversity at high temperatures where barriers are surmountable, then resampling to concentrate population on promising basins as temperature decreases. GPU-accelerated implementations (Barash, Weigel & Janke, 2017–2019) handle thousands of Potts spins.

The LP-evaluation budget constrains PA implementation: with R = 100 replicas, 30 temperature steps, and 5 sweeps per temperature, each sweep proposing N = 100 single-pair flips, you need ~1.5 million LP solves. This is feasible only if **incremental LP re-solves** (warm-starting from the previous basis after a single constraint flip) cost milliseconds rather than seconds. If each LP solve truly costs seconds, PA requires restricting to very small populations (R ≈ 5–10) with coarse temperature schedules.

**Kolmogorov complexity sampling** (Dingle & Hutter, Entropy, February 2026) provides an intriguing theoretical result: optima of combinatorial problems defined by simple objective functions tend to have **low Kolmogorov complexity**. They propose sampling according to algorithmic probability P(x) ∝ 2^{−K(x)}. Since Kolmogorov complexity is uncomputable, practical implementation uses compression proxies (Lempel-Ziv complexity, gzip length). For your 4-state Potts assignment, a "simple" solution might exhibit regular patterns in separation directions. Implementation is trivial (1–2 days: compute compression length of candidate assignments, add a bias term to Metropolis acceptance), but the payoff is speculative — no empirical validation exists for placement-type problems.

The **instanton path** concept is the elegant bridge between Frameworks 2 and 3: the instanton is the saddle-point of the Feynman path integral action S[γ] = Σ_t E(γ(t)), which is exactly the mountain pass from Framework 2. Smelyanskiy et al. (2016) developed instanton calculus for quantum spin models, confirming that QMC escape rates match quantum tunneling rates via dominant instantonic paths. However, their mean-field approach requires permutation symmetry (fully connected models). **No algorithmic implementation exists for finding instantons in general discrete combinatorial problems.** The concept remains a theoretical organizing principle rather than a practical algorithm.

## What actually works for VLSI placement today

The empirical landscape is clear and somewhat humbling for physics-inspired methods. **Simulated annealing with good move operators remains the gold standard for macro placement.** The TILOS-AI-Institute MacroPlacement benchmark (updated January 2026) shows that an improved SA baseline with multithreading and go-with-the-winners resampling achieves up to **26% better proxy cost** than Google's Circuit Training (AlphaChip) RL approach, using only one-quarter of the resources. SA wins 16 of 17 HPWL comparisons on ICCAD04 benchmarks. Markov's reassessment (CACM 2024) confirms that ML-based techniques lag behind analytical placers.

DREAMPlace (Lin et al., DAC 2019) dominates standard-cell placement via GPU-accelerated analytical optimization. **Hybro** (arXiv 2402.18311, 2024) directly addresses the local-minima problem by alternating between DREAMPlace gradient optimization and black-box perturbation — the closest published system to what you are building. For the ~30-evaluation budget specifically, **Bayesian optimization over combinatorial structures** (BOCS, Baptista & Poloczek, ICML 2018; AutoDMP, Agnesina et al., ISPD 2023) is designed exactly for expensive black-box discrete optimization. AutoDMP already wraps DREAMPlace with multi-objective BO for macro placement parameter tuning.

Adaptive Parallel Tempering with non-local cluster moves (Nature Communications 2025) establishes the strongest known classical baseline for spin-glass-type problems, outperforming both SQA and D-Wave quantum annealers on 3D Ising glasses. Simulated Bifurcation (Goto et al., Science Advances 2021) scales to **one million binary variables** on Max-Cut problems, making it the largest-scale physics-inspired optimizer demonstrated, though it operates on QUBO formulations rather than LP-based objectives.

## A practical synthesis: the dual-guided discrete GAD algorithm

The three frameworks converge on a single implementable algorithm. Here is a concrete 6-week plan, ranked by impact per implementation effort:

**Weeks 1–2: Discrete GAD with LP dual guidance (Framework 2 core).** At a local minimum σ₀, solve the LP and extract dual variables. Rank all macro pairs by |dual|. Flip the pair with smallest |dual| to get σ₁. Re-solve LP. If cost increased, continue flipping soft pairs (ascending toward the saddle). When cost begins decreasing, switch to greedy descent. This uses ~2 LP solves per step, fitting ~15 steps within the 30-evaluation budget. The Mountain Pass Theorem guarantees a saddle exists; GAD theory says the softest-mode ascent finds it most efficiently; reduced cost fixing confirms small duals identify flexible variables.

**Weeks 2–3: Parametric LP sweeps (Framework 1 residue).** For each macro pair at the local minimum, perform a single-parameter parametric LP sweep rotating its separation constraint (L→R or A→B). Track the optimal basis through boundary crossings. This probes the energy landscape along "rotation paths" between neighboring polyhedra at a cost of one LP pivot per basis change — potentially much cheaper than a full LP solve. Pairs whose parametric sweeps reveal decreasing-cost trajectories are promising flip candidates.

**Weeks 3–4: Population Annealing with warm-start LP (Framework 3 practical).** If incremental LP re-solves (warm-starting from a previous basis after a single-pair flip) can be done in milliseconds, implement PA with a small population (R = 10–30) and coarse temperature schedule (20 steps). Resample at each temperature, apply 3–5 Metropolis sweeps per replica. The LP-dual-guided move selection from weeks 1–2 can serve as the MCMC proposal distribution, biasing flips toward soft pairs.

**Weeks 4–5: Solution-space smoothing (Framework 1 continuation).** Implement a continuation schedule: start with a relaxed problem (subset of macros, looser constraints), solve to optimality, add macros or tighten constraints incrementally. Each relaxed problem is itself an LP. This is the practical VLSI implementation of Gaussian homotopy — tested on MCNC benchmarks by Dong et al. (2003) and applicable to any floorplanning representation.

**Week 6: Bayesian surrogate over assignment space.** Build a simple surrogate model (sparse Bayesian linear regression with pairwise monomial features, following BOCS) from all LP evaluations accumulated in weeks 1–5. Use the surrogate to predict which distant assignments might have low cost, and spend remaining LP evaluations verifying the best surrogate predictions. Use LP dual information to construct informative priors — pairs with large duals should have fixed features in the surrogate.

## Maturity assessment and failure modes

| Method | Maturity | Demonstrated scale | Failure mode | 6-week effort |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| LP-dual-guided discrete GAD | **Novel** (no prior work) | Untested | Soft-pair flips may lead to infeasible regions; saddle may be far | 2 weeks |
| Parametric LP sweeps | **Textbook** (theory since 1955) | 10K+ constraints (MPC) | Many basis changes may exceed budget; A-matrix changes harder | 2 weeks |
| Population Annealing | **Established** (benchmarked 2015+) | 512 spins (3D Ising) | Requires cheap incremental LP; wide barriers need high T | 3 weeks |
| Solution-space smoothing | **Established** (VLSI, 2003) | MCNC benchmarks | Relaxed solutions may not guide to good full solutions | 1 week |
| Bayesian surrogate (BOCS) | **Established** (ICML 2018) | 50–100 binary variables | Surrogate inaccurate in 500-D; pairwise features miss higher-order | 1 week |
| Discrete Schrödinger Bridges | **Recent/speculative** | 256 Potts variables | Needs 10⁵+ evaluations; LP cost prohibitive | Not feasible |
| Homotopy continuation (ℂ^{2N}) | **Textbook** (theory) but **infeasible** at scale | 20 variables max | Exponential path count | Not feasible |
| SQA / quantum tunneling | **Established** but **wrong regime** | 1000+ qubits (D-Wave) | Wide barriers defeat tunneling exponentially | Not recommended |

The **highest-risk, highest-reward** component is the discrete GAD — it has no precedent and could either find dramatically better solutions (if the soft-mode hypothesis is correct for your specific energy landscape) or fail to escape (if the local minimum is surrounded by a uniformly high barrier with no soft direction). The **lowest-risk** component is Population Annealing, which is proven on similar problems but may be bottlenecked by LP-solve cost. The **most underappreciated** component is parametric LP sweeps, which exploit classical LP theory to probe the landscape at sub-LP cost per step.

## Conclusion

The three frameworks are not independent alternatives but complementary views of a single phenomenon. Complexification says paths between basins exist in an enlarged space. The Mountain Pass Theorem says the optimal such path crosses at the lowest saddle. Quantum tunneling says the transition rate depends on the saddle's action. All three point to the same operational strategy: **find soft degrees of freedom (small LP duals) and navigate through them.**

The genuinely novel contribution here is the synthesis: connecting LP dual magnitudes to GAD's softest mode, to backbone theory's unfrozen variables, to survey propagation's joker spins. This connection appears in no single paper, though all ingredients are individually well-established. If validated empirically, the LP-dual-guided discrete GAD could be a significant methodological advance for escaping local minima in structured combinatorial optimization — not just VLSI placement but any problem where the objective decomposes into an LP over combinatorial assignments. The 30-evaluation budget is the binding constraint. It rules out all sampling-heavy methods and demands that every LP solve be maximally informative, which is precisely what dual-guided navigation provides: each solve reveals not just a cost but a complete sensitivity map of the local landscape.
